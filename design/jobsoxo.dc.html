The workflow

Inquiry to reconciled, without leaving.

Six stages that most shops run in six different tools, three of which are spreadsheets.

01

Inquiry

Every lead lands in one inbox with its site, contact and history attached. Nothing lives in someone's texts.

02

Estimating

Takeoffs against your own labor and material rates, priced consistently whoever writes them.

03

Scheduling

Crews, sites and days on one board, so you can see who is double-booked before Monday.

04

Invoicing & payment

Progress billing off the schedule, sent, tracked and chased without a separate reminder system.

05

Payroll

Hours logged on site flow straight to payroll and to the job's cost, at the same time.

06

Reconciliation

Books that close because the numbers were right when they were entered, not fixed afterwards.

Why it exists

We wrote it because nothing else fit.

Conor Foy Plaster has been finishing rooms in New York since 2002. By the time we were running several crews across several sites, the estimating lived in one place, the schedule in another, the hours on paper, and the truth about whether a job made money arrived three months late.

The construction software we tried was built for people who build buildings, not for shops who finish them. So Heidi Williams-Foy — Conor's wife — built ours. We have been running the business on it since the start of this year, and it opens to other shops in mid-September 2026.

Built for
Job-based service companies

Painting, plastering, decorating, flooring, tile, drywall — trades where a job has a scope, a crew and a schedule, and the margin lives in the labor.

Not built for
General contractors managing 40 subs

There is good software for that already, and this is not trying to be it.

Runs on
Phones on site, laptops in the office

Hours, photos and daily logs go in from the job. Everything else happens at a desk.
